The corner shop on 13th

Nell opened Tubwell in 2014 in a 1920s corner shop in Sellwood, after ten years as a vet tech. It’s still one room of tables, a room of tubs and a small room just for cats.

Twelve years on the doorframe

The building was a shoe repair for forty years. Nell painted the walls herself, bought one table, and borrowed a dryer from the clinic she’d just left.

Every time something changed, somebody marked it on the back-room doorframe in pencil, the way you’d mark a kid growing. The frame has been painted around ever since.

Three rooms and a counter

The plan that hangs by the front door. Dogs, cats and muddy dogs each get their own room, so nobody has to share the noise.

  1. Hand-over and pickup happen here. Five minutes at drop-off to check coat, skin, ears and nails, and the shop shelves behind the till.

  2. Three tables, one pet on each, and never more pets than groomers. Dogs are dried by hand with quiet velocity dryers. We don’t own a cage dryer.

  3. Its own door and its own table, and no dogs, ever. Cats come in on Tuesday and Thursday mornings, when the salon is at its quietest.

  4. Three waist-high tubs with ramps, warm water and a velocity dryer at each. Muddy dogs come straight in through the side door on Tenino.

  5. Laundry, the towel shelves, the fridge covered in your texts, and the doorframe with twelve years of pencil marks.
